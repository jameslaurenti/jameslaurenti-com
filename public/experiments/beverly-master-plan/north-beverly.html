Beverly's master plan · North Beverly

The plan said to study North Beverly first. A project got there first.

Beverly's 2021 master plan saw the shopping plazas by the North Beverly train station as a place that could someday become a walkable neighborhood. It told the city to study the area and update the rules before that happened. The study hasn't started. Now 179 apartments are proposed on the Stop & Shop site.

What is a master plan, and does the city have to follow it?

State law requires every city to write one. Beverly's, called PlanBeverly, was shaped by public meetings in every ward. The Planning Board adopted it in December 2020, and it took effect January 1, 2021.

It is a guide, not a law. Builders follow the zoning rules, not the plan. The plan only takes effect when the city changes its zoning to match it.

Why the plan called this "long-term"

The stores were busy and trains to North Beverly were infrequent, so the plan judged that "near-term development in this area is not likely." It still told the city to "put policies in place now that preserve the option for transit oriented development in the future" (p. 33).

It expected any change to come on the land zoned General Commercial, the tinted parcels on the map below, which includes the Stop & Shop site.

What the study was supposed to cover, and what was dropped

The plan's next step was to "initiate a neighborhood planning study" covering scale and character, traffic bottlenecks, zoning and design rules for a walkable area, using new development to build "a denser street network" and open space, and energy (p. 40).

The consultant's December 2019 draft was firmer for the plaza sites: raise the height limit slightly to four stories, loosen lot-size and setback rules, and use new development to add streets (presentation pp. 28 to 29). In the final plan these became long-term ideas and a study to come.

At the October 27, 2026 hearing, Beverly's Planning Director said the city had meant to do a North Beverly plan within ten years and "didn't get there" before a project arrived (Planning Board video, about 1:42). A Ward 5 resident who served on the plan's steering committee wrote that the street grid and wider zoning changes "were dropped from the final plan."

    5 · What we take from it

    Three takeaways

    1. The order got reversed. The plan asked for a study and new rules first. Instead, the first big project is being judged under the old rules, with its height handled as an exception.

    2. Right place, different shape. Apartments by the train with less parking is what the plan wanted here. One large building on one lot, with two driveways, is not the network of walkable streets it described.

    3. There's still time for the rest. The other tinted plaza sites can still be shaped. Starting the study before the next one is proposed would put the plan's order back.

    This is James Laurenti's reading, not the city's or A Better Beverly's. Whether something "fits" is judged against the plan's own words, with page numbers so you can check.
