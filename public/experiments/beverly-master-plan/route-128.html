Beverly's master plan · Route 128

Most of Beverly's new homes are being approved along Route 128. The plan pictured something else there.

Beverly's 2021 master plan treated the office parks and open land along Route 128 as places to keep for jobs and nature, and pointed new homes toward the train stations. Since then, about two out of three new homes approved or proposed in Beverly are here.

What is a master plan, and does the city have to follow it?

State law requires every city to write one. Beverly's, called PlanBeverly, was shaped by public meetings in every ward. The Planning Board adopted it in December 2020, and it took effect January 1, 2021.

It is a guide, not a law. Builders follow the zoning rules, not the plan. The plan only takes effect when the city changes its zoning to match it.

1 · What the plan wanted here

Jobs on the business land, woods left as woods

▦

Keep the business parks for jobs

Cherry Hill, Dunham Rd, Sohier and Tozer Rd and the airport: hold this land for offices, labs and manufacturing (pp. 36, 41, 76).

↑

Make them work better

Allow restaurants and services for workers, taller buildings away from homes, and less parking (p. 41).

○

Leave open land alone

Discourage building on woods and fields, and send homes to the train stations instead (pp. 36, 43).

The plan's reasoning

As old industrial land downtown turns into housing, the plan wanted other areas held for business "in order to encourage a strong commercial sector" (p. 36). Beverly taxes business property at a higher rate than homes (p. 72).

On open land, it says building "in undeveloped areas of Beverly contributes to traffic congestion and loss of open space, and is more costly for the City to support" (p. 36).

    5 · What we take from it

    Three takeaways

    1. The biggest decisions happened where the plan had the least say. Folly Hill's rules were locked in by a 2016 filing, before the plan existed. The plan had no way to steer it.

    2. Senior housing is the common path. A zoning rule allows senior housing in almost every district with a permit. Dunham Ridge and the Cummings Center project both used it, and Boulder Ln is aiming to.

    3. Business land is turning into housing one permit at a time. The plan wanted that land kept for jobs. The city hasn't made a citywide choice about trading it for homes; it is happening case by case.

    This is James Laurenti's reading, not the city's or A Better Beverly's. Whether something "fits" is judged against the plan's own words, with page numbers so you can check.
