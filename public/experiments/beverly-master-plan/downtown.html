Beverly's master plan · Downtown

Beverly made a plan for downtown. How is it going?

In 2021 the city adopted a master plan, a guide for how Beverly should grow over 10 to 15 years. Downtown, near the train, is where it wanted growth to start. Here is what it asked for, and what has happened since.

What is a master plan, and does the city have to follow it?

State law requires every city to write one. Beverly's, called PlanBeverly, was shaped by public meetings in every ward. The Planning Board adopted it in December 2020, and it took effect January 1, 2021.

It is a guide, not a law. Builders follow the zoning rules, not the plan. The plan only takes effect when the city changes its zoning to match it. That's why this page looks at both: the projects, and the rule changes.

1 · The big idea

Grow where the town already works

→

Build near the train and the shops

New homes and businesses go where there are already streets, pipes, buses and trains.

○

Protect open land

Fewer new buildings on woods and fields at the edge of town.

≡

Make the rules clear

Simple, predictable zoning, so good projects don't need special favors.

The plan's own words

The vision is "an attractive, welcoming, and sustainable community" with zoning that is "balanced and inclusive" (p. 12). Two of its eight principles carry this idea: meet the housing needs of all residents, and focus redevelopment in existing centers (p. 13).

It says growth near transit "is essential to achieving the City's sustainability goals" and calls growth "vital to the City, since it provides revenue" (p. 7).

    4 · Where the new homes went

    Downtown is doing what the plan hoped. But most new homes are being approved elsewhere.

    Each square is 1% of the 1,005 new homes approved, or waiting on a decision, across Beverly since 2021.

    16% downtown. Small apartment buildings near the train, almost all a good fit with the plan.
    84% elsewhere. The biggest: 440 apartments on wooded land at Folly Hill, away from the train stations.
    How well does each new home fit the plan, citywide?
    • Doesn't fit: 440 apartments at Folly Hill, on open land away from the stations. Its zoning was locked in by a 2016 filing, before the plan existed.
    • Partly fits: 179 apartments proposed at 37 Enon St by the North Beverly station (still under review), 160 senior apartments approved in 2022 for the Dunham Rd office park (not built), and a few small projects.
    • Fits: downtown's projects, plus 59 senior apartments at the Cummings Center.

    5 · What we take from it

    Three takeaways

    1. The projects fit; the volume doesn't. Downtown's new buildings look like what the plan described. But downtown is getting about one in six of the city's new homes.

    2. The rules moved the other way. The plan asked for clear rules to replace a special height allowance near the train. The allowance went away, the new rules never came, and heights there went down.

    3. The first priority hasn't started. Bass River, next to the train station, was the plan's near-term goal. The rezoning was paused in 2022 and is expected to restart in 2027.

    This is James Laurenti's reading, not the city's or A Better Beverly's. Whether something "fits" is judged against the plan's own words, with page numbers so you can check.
